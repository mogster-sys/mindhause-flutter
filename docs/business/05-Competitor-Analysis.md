# MindHause — Competitor Analysis

## 1. Frame

Three concentric circles of competition:
1. **Direct** — productivity apps positioned for personal task / life management
2. **Indirect** — adjacent tools targeting the same outcome (memory, focus, life-organisation) via different paradigms
3. **Adjacent** — apps the audience would substitute on a slow day (cosy games, journalling, meditation)

We win not by being a better Todoist. We win by occupying a position **none of them can move to without breaking their model**.

---

## 2. Direct competitors

### 2.1 Todoist (Doist)
- **Model**: Freemium + subscription ($4-5/mo Premium, $8/mo Business)
- **UI**: Mature list-with-projects, labels, filters
- **Sync**: Cloud + accounts required
- **Stars**: App Store 4.8 / Play 4.6, tens of millions of installs
- **Strengths**: Polished, deep integrations (calendar, voice, Slack), Karma gamification, natural language input, mature
- **Weaknesses**: Flat list (the whole category's wall), subscription friction, cloud account required even for solo use, ADHD users frequently report bouncing off it
- **Where MindHause wins**: spatial paradigm, no subscription, no account, ADHD-specific affordances. Direct addressable swap for the user who's already tried Todoist and abandoned.

### 2.2 Things 3 (Cultured Code)
- **Model**: One-time purchase per platform ($9.99 iOS / $49.99 Mac / $19.99 iPad)
- **UI**: Genuinely beautiful, Apple-design-award winner
- **Sync**: Apple-only ecosystem (no Windows/Android), proprietary Cultured Code Cloud
- **Strengths**: Best-of-breed UX in flat-list category, one-time price model proves customers will pay
- **Weaknesses**: Still a list. iOS-only ecosystem. No specific ADHD affordances. No 3D/spatial. No theming.
- **Where MindHause wins**: cross-platform (Android + iOS), spatial novelty, theming, ADHD focus. Things 3 *proves* the one-time-purchase model works — we follow that pricing playbook with a different paradigm.

### 2.3 Notion (Notion Labs)
- **Model**: Freemium + subscription ($10/mo Personal Pro, $15/mo Plus, AI add-on)
- **UI**: Document-database hybrid, infinitely flexible
- **Sync**: Cloud-mandatory, account-mandatory
- **Strengths**: Power, customisation, second-brain capability, large community
- **Weaknesses**: Notion-tinkering becomes the work; high abandonment among casual users; the "I made a beautiful system and never used it" cohort is enormous; subscription stack; cloud-first
- **Where MindHause wins**: opinionated structure (no tinkering required), spatial constraint as a feature, privacy-first, no subscription, doesn't reward perfectionism. The user who has burned out on Notion-tinkering is a high-value prospect.

### 2.4 Apple Reminders / Google Tasks (the defaults)
- **Model**: Free (bundled with OS)
- **UI**: Native, instant, frictionless capture
- **Sync**: iCloud / Google account
- **Strengths**: Free, native, fast capture, voice integration, present by default on the device
- **Weaknesses**: Nothing beyond lists. No structure for goals, projects, or context. No engagement layer.
- **Where MindHause wins**: structure, context, novelty, ADHD-fit. The user who has "Apple Reminders works fine for me" is NOT our prospect; the user who has Apple Reminders + Things + Todoist + Notion all installed and uses none is.

### 2.5 TickTick
- **Model**: Freemium + subscription ($35.99/year Premium)
- **UI**: List + calendar + Pomodoro + habits, light gamification (streak/score)
- **Sync**: Cloud + account
- **Strengths**: Feature-dense, good Pomodoro integration, habit tracking included
- **Weaknesses**: Visual noise (feature-density without information hierarchy); gamification bolted on, not designed-in; subscription; cloud-first
- **Where MindHause wins**: cleaner conceptual model (rooms = contexts, objects = tasks); gamification as integral metaphor (cat + monsters) not bolt-on; privacy/pricing

### 2.6 Habitica
- **Model**: Freemium + subscription ($4.99/mo)
- **UI**: RPG-themed habit/task tracking — characters, gold, pets, parties
- **Sync**: Web + account
- **Strengths**: Strong community, full-on gamification, low cost
- **Weaknesses**: Web-first, dated UI, requires significant maintenance to keep playing, account-mandatory
- **Where MindHause wins**: native mobile + immersive 3D; gamification serves the task system (monsters = neglected tasks, cat = soft AI assistant) rather than tasks serving the game; no daily-streak pressure that's anti-ADHD

### 2.7 Motion / Reclaim / Sunsama (AI scheduling)
- **Model**: Subscription only ($19-34/mo)
- **UI**: AI auto-scheduled calendar
- **Sync**: Cloud + account + heavy calendar integration
- **Strengths**: Removes scheduling cognitive load; promising for high-volume professional workflows
- **Weaknesses**: Expensive; opaque (you don't know why something moved); calendar-tied (assumes time-block worker); needs cloud access to your calendar (privacy-sensitive users won't accept); not designed for ADHD
- **Where MindHause wins**: privacy, price, ADHD audience, spatial affordance for context-switching

## 3. Indirect competitors

### 3.1 Memory palace apps (the literal category)
- **Anki**, **Memrise**, **Quizlet** — spaced-repetition flashcards. Some users build memory palaces inside Anki manually.
- **Loci / Magnetic Memory Method tools** — niche apps for the technique itself
- **None are productivity apps.** They're learning/memorisation tools. MindHause is the first to apply the technique to *task management* rather than memorisation. Adjacent positioning, not competition.

### 3.2 Focus / pomodoro apps
- **Forest**, **Focus@Will**, **Be Focused**
- One-shot focus session apps. MindHause includes a Focus Mode (palace dimmed, single task surfaced) but isn't competing on the pomodoro vertical.

### 3.3 Journalling apps
- **Day One**, **Stoic**, **Reflectly**
- Adjacent, not competing — but the engine architecturally supports a journalling layer in a future expansion (a Library shelf for journal entries, a Garden meditation prompt, etc.)

### 3.4 Mind-mapping / second-brain
- **MindNode**, **Obsidian**, **Roam Research**, **Logseq**
- Knowledge-organisation tools; serve "thinking" more than "doing". MindHause's Library room is the doorway to a future module here.

## 4. Adjacent / substitute attention

### 4.1 Cosy mobile games
- **Stardew Valley**, **A Short Hike**, **Unpacking**, **Animal Crossing**, **Pocket Build**, **Townscaper**
- Compete for *moments of mobile attention* in a similar emotional register (warm, ambient, low-pressure). Not productivity apps, but the audience overlaps significantly with the ADHD/cosy-game-crossover segment.
- **MindHause borrows the feel** while delivering utility. This is a real differentiator — *no cosy game has a calendar; no productivity app feels like a cosy game*.

### 4.2 Meditation / mindfulness
- **Calm**, **Headspace**, **Insight Timer**, **Oak**
- Subscription-heavy, content-driven. The McKenna 9-square Support Circle (via the DecisionLens integration roadmap) brings a piece of this surface into MindHause without competing as a meditation app.

## 7. Switching cost / lock-in considerations

A competitor moat is also about switching cost. MindHause is **honest about portability**:
- All data is in plain on-device SQLite — users can export at any time
- No proprietary cloud format
- Theme packs are content, not lock-in features

This is unusual (most apps optimise for lock-in). The bet: **trust is the moat**, and honest portability deepens trust faster than artificial friction would.

---

## 8. Honest threats to the analysis

1. **Apple or Google bundles something similar into the OS**. Mitigation: even Apple Reminders isn't ADHD-specific or 3D; OS-bundled apps don't typically reach the depth MindHause does. But if they do, our differentiation narrows.
2. **A well-funded competitor (e.g. a productivity company we don't see coming) builds the spatial paradigm**. Mitigation: we have engineering lead, the privacy/pricing stance is still defensible, and the cosy-game DNA is hard to fake.
3. **The market simply doesn't value the spatial paradigm at scale** — i.e. people prefer lists. Mitigation: the ADHD segment is enough to build a profitable business at modest scale even if the wider productivity market shrugs. Validate this hypothesis with v1.

---

## 9. Conclusion

MindHause occupies a defensible position that established competitors structurally cannot follow. The win condition is:
1. Reach the ADHD audience at the right depth (marketing plan, channels, voice)
2. Validate the spatial paradigm's appeal beyond ADHD (the secondary/tertiary segments)
3. Compound the engine as a platform (DecisionLens integration, future adjacent layers) so the engineering substrate becomes increasingly difficult to replicate

If those three hold, the white space is ours to occupy and widen.
